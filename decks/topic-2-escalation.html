AC Scenario

The scenario, set in concrete.

Customer "Acme Co." — top-decile commerce account, ~$1.2 M ARR. Their storefront-search hub is intermittently failing under Black Friday traffic. Their CEO has emailed our CRO. We are now twelve hours from doors-open in EU and twenty hours from US.

  • Symptoms. 5xx rate spiking to ~14 % during peak minutes; p95 latency 4.2 s vs. our 250 ms SLO; relevance complaints from agents on chat.
  • Business impact. Estimated $40k/min in lost cart-conversion at peak. Their team has already drafted a postmortem talking-point: "Coveo can't handle our scale."
  • The first call. Their CTO at 21:47 UTC. The CRO loops in our CEO.
02
AC Frame

How I run the room.

Five acts. Each one has a single job, a single deliverable, and a clock.

  • Triage. Stop the bleed. 0 → 15 min. Deliverable: war room, severity, single number we anchor on.
  • Root cause. Hypothesis tree, telemetry, repro. 15 min → 2 hrs. Deliverable: the cause, with evidence we can show.
  • Stabilize. Short-term remediation. Parallel to RCA. Deliverable: degraded-but-stable.
  • Communicate. Cadenced, structured, exec-readable. From minute one until close. Deliverable: trust.
  • Prevent. Postmortem, prevention plan, customer-facing 60-day commitment. Deliverable: it doesn't happen again.
03
AC Act I · Triage

Stop the bleed in 15 minutes.

  • Severity gate, not severity guess. Customer-facing 5xx > 5 % for > 5 min on a top-decile account = SEV-1, no debate. SEV is set by the on-call FDE, not negotiated.
  • One war room. Slack + bridge. I'm the IC. Roles named on first message: scribe, customer liaison, exec liaison, technical lead. Anyone unnamed is a witness, not a participant.
  • One number. The whole room anchors on a single metric — for this incident, customer-side 5xx rate on storefront-search. We don't argue about p99, ML scores, click-through, anything else, until it's flat.
  • The first 5-minute sweep. Have we shipped anything in the last 24 h? Has the customer? Did a model just retrain? Did source-build status change? Three yes/no questions narrow the hypothesis tree in seconds.

AC Act II · RCA

The hypothesis tree, named ahead of time.

I refuse to "investigate widely" during a SEV-1. I run a hypothesis tree the team has rehearsed, and I close branches as fast as evidence lets me.

                   "search is failing"
                            │
        ┌───────────────────┼───────────────────┐
   1. CAPACITY         2. DATA              3. CONFIG
   • QPS regression   • bad source build   • pipeline statement
   • runner pool      • broken mapping     • ML model swap
   • upstream LB      • encoding shift     • search hub change
                            │
                       4. DEPENDENCY
                       • CDN
                       • auth provider
                       • Coveo platform itself

For each branch, I keep one telemetry pane and one repro recipe in the runbook. Branch-close criteria are also pre-written.

06
AC Act II · RCA

The telemetry stack we expected to need.

Coveo-side

  • Search Usage Analytics — QPS by search hub, error-code histogram, p50/p95/p99.
  • Source health — last successful build, document delta, indexer queue depth.
  • Pipeline + ML status — last statement edit, last model retrain, model "Active" vs "Building."
  • API latency — /search/v2, /search/v3/passages.

Customer-side

  • Real-User Monitoring on the storefront search box.
  • Edge logs at their CDN — request, status, geo, retry.
  • App logs wrapping the Coveo client (request ID, retry count, fallback fired).
  • Business KPI — cart-creation rate, the "did it actually move money" gate.

If the telemetry isn't joined on a request ID end-to-end, I treat that as a P1 follow-up regardless of root cause. You can't run RCA on hearsay.

AC Act III · Stabilize

Short-term remediation, ranked by reversibility.

MoveReversibilityCustomer costWhen I reach for it
Pin pipeline to last-known-good revisionInstantNone visibleDefault — first move on suspected statement/model regression.
Rollback an ML model snapshotInstantRelevance dips for ~hoursIf the model retrained right before the spike.
Bump cache TTL at the edgeInstantStale results for ~minIf the platform itself is degraded — buys us breathing room.
Shed traffic via query throttlingManualSome users see retriesOnly when capacity is the actual cause.
Failover search-hub to a secondary pipelineManualDifferent rankingIf a single pipeline is poisoned but the index is fine.
Cut over to /search/v2 from /v3/passagesManualLoses Passage Retrieval featuresIf a v3-only path is the broken one and v2 is healthy.

Reach for the most reversible move first. Always.

AC Act IV · Comms

Comms is a feature, not an afterthought.

During SEV-1, the customer's confidence in us is itself a service that needs an SLO.

  • Cadence ladder. Customer technical lead = every 15 min. Customer CTO/CIO = every 30 min. Customer CEO/CRO = every hour. The cadence is set in writing in message #1.
  • Format ladder. Slack thread for the technical lead. Email + Slack for the CTO. Email-only for the CEO. Different surfaces, same source of truth.
  • Format SIAN. Situation · Impact · Action · Next update. Four headers. No prose drift.
  • What we say to the CEO is not what we say to the CTO. The CEO needs to know I'm an adult. The CTO needs to know I'm right.

AC Act IV · Comms

What we say internally is different.

I am unsparing internally and humble externally. Two different audiences, two different sets of words, the same set of facts.

To Coveo execs (real-time)

  • SEV-1, top-decile account, customer-CEO involvement.
  • Estimated revenue impact at the customer (signal for our exposure).
  • What I've already done, what I'm about to do.
  • What I need them to do (or, more often: please don't call this customer right now — let me run the room).

To the FDE / TS team (real-time)

  • Single Slack thread. Append-only. Time-stamped.
  • One channel for IC commands; another for chatter. Don't mix.
  • "What did we just learn?" check every 15 minutes.
  • End-of-incident: a 24-hour rule — postmortem draft within 24 h, blameless, named contributors named.

AC Act V · Prevent

The 60-day post-incident commitment, on the customer side.

  • Joint capacity model. Their UA-derived peak QPS, our headroom, our SLO. One spreadsheet, owned jointly, refreshed quarterly.
  • Pre-event runbook. Each of their named peak events (Black Friday, Cyber Monday, Boxing Day, mid-season) gets a one-page runbook. Pin pipeline. Freeze ML retrain. Synthetic load test. Pager rotation.
  • Joint GameDay. Twice a year, we deliberately break their staging pipeline together. Their on-call answers a real page. Their CTO sees it work.
  • Shared OKRs. Their CSAT on search, our SLO compliance, our time-to-mitigate, our time-to-RCA. One dashboard, owned jointly, reviewed monthly.
"We don't sell uptime. We sell trust. Uptime is just one of trust's downstream effects."
13
AC Act V · Prevent

The systemic prevention plan, on our side.

  • Error budgets per search hub. Burn the budget = an automatic ML retrain freeze + paged review. The platform refuses to be put into an unsafe state.
  • Pipeline revision pinning as a feature. Customer-facing API: POST /pipelines/:id/pin?revision=…. Reverting a config has to be cheaper than calling me.
  • "FDE on call" rotation. Not just engineering. A senior FDE is paged into every customer-facing SEV-1 from minute zero. The named-customer relationship is itself part of the mitigation.
  • Runbook library as code. Every customer's peak-event runbook lives in Git, is reviewed quarterly, and is rehearsed on a GameDay. If it isn't rehearsed, it doesn't exist.
  • Customer Success partnership. Every SEV-1 generates a postmortem that the named CSM owns the customer-facing version of. Engineering owns the technical version. Both versions ship in 24 h. The CSM is in the war room from minute one — that's the muscle most vendors miss.
